# PRD 011: Revolving credit and card statements

## Status
Draft

## Objective

Let a tenant track **revolving credit** — credit cards and rotating lines such
as ADDI or Flamingo — where the ceiling is fixed and what is owed each month
depends on what was bought.

PRD-007 models a debt that shrinks on a plan. This one models a debt that grows
with use and is settled against a bill the lender issues.

Upon completion of this PRD, a user must be able to:
- record a credit line and the limit it carries
- enter a statement and see its payment among the month's obligations
- see how much of each limit is still available
- see what the household owes on revolving credit, apart from what it holds and
  apart from what its installment plans carry

---

## Problem

PRD-007 deferred this deliberately, and named what was missing: *"a limit, a
statement cycle, a minimum payment, and a balance that grows with use rather
than shrinking on a schedule."*

The spreadsheet Plinto replaces carries it in two places. Each monthly block has
a `CREDITOS ONLINE` line that sums the `CREDITOS <MES>` sub-tables, and ADDI
appears as a single aggregated monthly figure — a household that holds several
rotating lines and pays a different amount on each of them every month.

Today the only way to record ADDI is a recurring rule with a fixed amount that
is wrong every month.

Without this PRD:
- a card is an account with a negative balance and no ceiling, so *how much can
  I still spend* has no answer in the system
- the payment must be re-entered by hand every month, or left wrong
- nothing separates **what is owed in total** from **what must be paid now** —
  two numbers the issuer prints side by side because they answer different
  questions

---

## Users

- **Owner / Member** of a tenant, who can record credit lines and enter their
  statements.
- **Viewer**, who can see lines and statements but cannot record either.
- The user already has a tenant (PRD 001), accounts and transactions
  (PRD 002/003) and the obligations engine (PRD 006), which this PRD extends
  rather than duplicates.

## Scope (In Scope)

### 1. A credit line

| Field | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| `name` | `ADDI`, `Visa Bancolombia` |
| `limitMinor` | the ceiling as it stands today |
| `currency` | |
| `status` | `active` \| `closed` |

Four fields. No cutoff day, no due day, no billing cycle — see *Decisions*.

A credit line is **not** an `Account` and does not appear among them. See
*Decisions* for why, since it is the call this PRD turns on.

### 2. A statement is the unit of truth

| Field | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| `creditLineId` | |
| `period` | `YYYY-MM`. Grouping only — never identity |
| `cutoffDate` | when this statement closed |
| `dueDate` | the **when** |
| `closingBalanceMinor` | total owed, as the issuer declares it |
| `amountDueMinor` | the **how much** |
| `limitMinorSnapshot` | the limit at that cutoff |

A statement carries its **own** dates and its **own** limit rather than
inheriting them from the line. Issuers move cutoffs and raise ceilings, and a
figure recomputed against today's limit would silently restate what March's
available credit had been. This is the principle PRD-006 already applies to
`expectedAmountMinor`: what a period was told, it was told.

### 3. A statement becomes an obligation

`ObligationSourceType` gains `credit_line`, alongside a nullable
`creditLineStatementId` and the same extended `CHECK` constraint pattern
PRD-007 established. No existing column changes.

**Idempotency is per statement, not per period**:
`@@unique([creditLineStatementId])`. This is the difference from
`debt_schedule`, and it is what lets a line bill twice inside one calendar
month without the second statement being refused.

The obligation carries the statement's `amountDueMinor` as its expected amount
and its `dueDate` as the due date. No translation, no derivation — the issuer
already answered both questions.

**Nothing is generated by a background job.** Rules and schedules can be
materialized ahead of time because they are predictable. A statement is not: it
exists when the lender issues it. The obligation is created when the statement
is entered, and the scheduler is untouched by this PRD.

### 4. Available credit

```
availableMinor = limitMinorSnapshot − closingBalanceMinor
```

From the most recent statement. **Derived, never stored**, and always presented
with the cutoff it came from — *"available at the 12 Jul cutoff"*. A currency
figure without a date invites the reader to believe it is current; this one
frequently is not, and says so.

It may be negative, and an over-limit balance is **not** blocked. Plinto records
what happened; policing the ceiling is the issuer's job, and fees push real
balances past it. A system that refuses to record a fact that already occurred
forces the household to lie to it.

### 5. Settling a statement

Unchanged from PRD-006: a transaction is linked to the obligation, and the
obligation's status is derived from its payments.

The payment leaves a bank account and **nothing is posted to the credit line**.
The next statement already arrives with the balance reduced, because the issuer
did that arithmetic. Posting it here as well would put a second source of truth
under a figure the lender declares, and the two would disagree the first time a
payment landed on either side of a cutoff.

### 6. What the household owes

The debt summary gains a **third figure per currency**, beside
`scheduledOutstandingMinor` and `lenderOwedMinor`:

- `revolvingOwedMinor` — the sum of the latest `closingBalanceMinor` across
  active lines.

PRD-007 established the discipline and the reason: figures that measure
different things, drawn from different sources, are reported apart. A single
headline would be a number nobody could defend.

### 7. What the household sees

A **Credits and cards** section, always visible while a line is `active`,
showing per line: the amount due, the available credit, and the total owed.

When no statement has been entered for the current period, the section shows the
**last known** figures, flagged as estimated and stamped with their cutoff date.
The line never disappears from view, and no row is invented to keep it there —
see *Decisions*.

An estimated figure is reported **beside** the period's payable total, never
inside it:

> A pagar 23.375.030 · más ~800.000 estimados en créditos

## Decisions

Four calls that shape the model, recorded here rather than left implicit.

### A credit line is not an account

The obvious design is an `Account` of type `credit` with a `creditLimitMinor`
column, where available credit falls out of the balance that already exists.

It was rejected because **the derived figure would require data this household
does not enter.** Available credit computed as *limit plus balance* only moves
when every purchase is posted as a transaction. This household records the
monthly bill, not the purchases behind it — so the balance would sit at zero,
and the line would report its full limit as available, every day, with the face
of a correct number.

A derived value drawn from data nobody enters is not a value. It is a lie with a
currency format.

The declared statement is therefore the source of truth for a revolving balance.
This also settles a question PRD-007 left open — *whether a purchase should
also post to the account it pays down* — for this half of the problem: **no**,
not for revolving credit.

There is a semantic argument underneath the practical one. A bank account is a
place money sits. A card is a **relationship with a ceiling that issues a bill**.
Modelling the second as the first is what makes "my accounts" a list the
household cannot read at a glance.

> **Alternative rejected.** `Account` + `creditLimitMinor` + a CHECK. One
> column instead of two tables, and correct for a household that logs every
> purchase. Wrong for this one, and wrong in a way that reports confidently.

`AccountType.credit` is **left in place**. A household that does want the full
ledger can still hold a card as a liability account, exactly as PRD-007 says it
can today, and removing an enum value in use buys nothing. The overlap is a
known cost of leaving both doors open, not an oversight.

### The statement is the identity, not the month

Following `debt_schedule` to the letter would give
`@@unique([creditLineId, period])` — one statement per line per month.

Some of these lenders let the household choose biweekly billing. Two statements
inside one calendar month, and that constraint refuses the second.

The fix is not a bigger constraint but a truer one. An obligation here does not
exist *because a month turned*. It exists *because a statement arrived*. Keyed
on the statement, cadence stops being something the model has an opinion about:
monthly, biweekly, or irregular all work, and `period` degrades to what it
should always have been in this context — a bucket for grouping the month's
board.

`listPeriod` and `getPeriodSummary` need no change. They work per row, not
one-per-source.

### No billing cycle is configured, and none is inferred

Four designs were considered and all four rejected: `cutoffDay` + `dueDay`;
`cutoffDays Int[]`; `cycleAnchorDate` + `cycleDays`; and inferring the cadence
from the cutoff dates already entered.

They all exist to answer one question — *when is the next statement due* — and
that question is only asked in order to create a placeholder obligation ahead of
time. Removing the placeholder removes the question, and with it every field
above.

Two further reasons they were poor answers regardless:

- **Monthly and every-N-days are different arithmetic.** Monthly is calendar
  (a cutoff on the 30th makes February 28 days long); N-day is an interval that
  drifts and produces months with three cutoffs. No single integer holds both,
  and a discriminator to choose between them is a field whose only job is to
  explain the other fields.
- **A configured cycle is where a typo lives.** Over the course of designing
  this, the same line was described as billing every 14 days and every 15 days.
  Both are valid values; no validation catches the wrong one; and every
  projection afterwards runs a day off, compounding. Observed cutoff dates
  cannot carry that error, because there is nowhere to type it.

> **Alternative rejected.** Inferring cadence from the last two cutoff dates.
> Self-correcting and needs no configuration — genuinely better than storing it
> — but it still exists only to serve the placeholder, and it needs two
> statements before it can say anything.

### The estimate is a view, not a row

The household does need to keep sight of a line whose statement has not arrived
yet. That requirement is real, and dropping it was rejected.

It is met by the dashboard section of §7, which shows the last known figures
with their date. It is **not** met by writing an obligation nobody was billed
for, dated by a guess.

The obligations table therefore holds only facts: statements that existed. There
is nothing to reconcile later between an invented row and the real one that
replaces it, and no cleanup when a line is closed mid-cycle.

> **Alternative rejected.** Generating a placeholder obligation carrying the
> previous statement's amount. It reads as the friendlier design, and it is the
> single requirement that dragged in every cadence field above. It also puts a
> row nobody entered into a table whose entire discipline is that its rows are
> facts.

## Out of Scope

- Interest, minimum-versus-total payment strategies, and what a revolving
  balance costs to carry
- Per-purchase detail on a revolving line. A household that wants the full
  ledger can hold the card as a `credit` account today
- Rewards, cashback, points
- Importing statements from the issuer, by file or by API
- Alerting that a statement is late or a payment is due
- A statement in a currency other than its line's (PRD-008)
- Loading the spreadsheet's `CREDITOS` sub-tables (PRD-010)

## Main Flow (Happy Path)

1. The household holds an ADDI line with a 1,200,000 limit.
2. They record the line once: name, limit, currency.
3. A statement arrives — cutoff 12 Jul, due 20 Jul, 800,000 owed in total,
   300,000 to pay.
4. Entering it creates one obligation for 300,000 due 20 Jul, and the line
   reports 400,000 available at the 12 Jul cutoff.
5. They pay from Bancolombia. The transaction is linked to the obligation, which
   reads `paid`; the bank balance drops by 300,000 and the line does not move.
6. August opens with no statement yet. ADDI still appears on the board with
   ~300,000 estimated and 400,000 available, both stamped *12 Jul*, and the
   estimate is reported beside the month's total rather than inside it.
7. The next statement closes on 26 Jul — fourteen days later, not thirty. It is
   entered like any other, a second obligation appears in the same month, and
   nothing in the system had to know the cycle changed.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] A credit line can be created with a limit, and does not appear among
      accounts.
- [ ] Entering a statement creates exactly one obligation, carrying the
      statement's amount due and due date.
- [ ] Two statements closing in the same calendar month create two obligations.
- [ ] Re-entering the same statement creates no duplicate obligation.
- [ ] Correcting a statement corrects its obligation, and cannot set an amount
      below what has already been paid against it.
- [ ] Available credit is computed against the limit recorded at that cutoff,
      not the line's current limit.
- [ ] Available credit may be negative, and an over-limit balance is recorded
      rather than refused.
- [ ] Paying a statement links a bank transaction to the obligation and leaves
      the credit line untouched.
- [ ] A line with no statement in the current period still appears, showing its
      last known figures and the cutoff they came from.
- [ ] An estimated figure is never summed into the period's payable total.
- [ ] Revolving debt is reported per currency, apart from scheduled debt and
      apart from lender balances.
- [ ] A viewer can see lines and statements and can record neither.
- [ ] Every credit line and statement write is audited with actor and
      correlation id.

## Success Metrics

- The household stops maintaining the `CREDITOS` sub-tables by hand.
- *How much can I still spend on this card* is answerable in Plinto, without
  opening the issuer's app.
- A card's monthly payment appears on the board without anybody recreating it.
- A lender switching a line from monthly to biweekly billing requires **no
  change in Plinto** — not a setting, not a migration, not a line of code.

## Technical Notes

- Persistence and multi-currency per ADR 0004, at the currency's real minor
  unit — a COP statement carries no centavos.
- **No background job.** Obligations are created on statement entry. This is a
  deliberate departure from PRD-006 and PRD-007, and the reason is in
  *Decisions*: a statement cannot be predicted, so there is nothing to schedule.
- Authorization per ADR 0007: `credit:read` / `credit:write`, granted on the
  same lines as `obligation:*` — a viewer reads, an owner and a member write.
- The `CHECK` constraint on `obligation_instances` is extended, not replaced.
  Prisma does not model CHECK constraints, so it lives in the migration only.
- **That extension needs its own migration**, for the reason PRD-007 documented:
  Postgres will not let a value added by `ALTER TYPE ... ADD VALUE` be *used*
  until the adding transaction commits, and Prisma runs each migration inside
  one. Comparing `source_type` against `'credit_line'` in the constraint is
  exactly that use.
- `@@unique([creditLineStatementId])` sits alongside the existing
  `(recurringRuleId, period)` and `(debtScheduleId, period)`. Postgres treats
  NULLs as distinct, so it constrains only the rows that carry one.
- Correcting a statement propagates to its obligation, unlike a rule, whose
  amount is snapshotted into each instance. A rule is a template for many
  periods; a statement and its obligation are **one fact recorded once**, and
  leaving them able to disagree would serve nobody.

### Endpoints

| Method | Path | Permission |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `GET` | `/api/credit-lines` | `credit:read` |
| `POST` | `/api/credit-lines` | `credit:write` |
| `PATCH` | `/api/credit-lines/{id}` | `credit:write` |
| `POST` | `/api/credit-lines/{id}/close` | `credit:write` |
| `GET` | `/api/credit-lines/{id}/statements` | `credit:read` |
| `POST` | `/api/credit-lines/{id}/statements` | `credit:write` |
| `PATCH` | `/api/credit-lines/{id}/statements/{statementId}` | `credit:write` |

Statements are nested under their line because one never exists without it, and
because `period` is not an identifier here — the flat route would have no
natural key to expose.

### Dependencies

**None beyond what is already delivered.** This PRD was scoped alongside a
separate change adding `PATCH /api/obligations/{id}` and an estimated-amount
flag for obligations whose value varies. Once the estimate became a view rather
than a row, the dependency dissolved: every obligation this PRD creates carries
a real amount from a real statement, and corrections travel through the
statement that produced it.

That other change stands on its own justification — utilities whose amount
varies month to month — and the two can be built in either order.

### Delivery

Two vertical slices, in order:

| Slice | Outcome |
| --- | --- |
| 11.1 | Record a credit line and its statements; the payment appears among the month's obligations |
| 11.2 | Available credit, revolving debt total, and the dashboard section |

11.1 is the bulk of the work and is where `credit_line` enters
`ObligationSourceType`.

### Forward compatibility

- **PRD-008 (multi-currency)** is where a statement in a currency other than its
  line's belongs, along with how it would enter a period total.
- **PRD-010 (spreadsheet import)** loads the `CREDITOS` sub-tables as statements.
  It depends on this PRD. Note that the sheet's sub-table titles are shifted by
  one month and must be resolved by cell reference rather than by label — see
  `apps/api/scripts/import-spreadsheet/README.md`, which documents that and
  three other ways the sheet misleads.
- **Per-purchase detail**, if it is ever wanted, is additive: a line could gain
  transactions without the statement model changing, and the two figures would
  then be reported apart for the same reason §6 already reports three.
